# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market that could be owned.** The niche is Indian-origin Canadians moving NRO money India → Canada. That direction is the one the named remittance apps mostly do not serve, and it is narrow enough for one focused player to take a large share. Whether any digital player already does it end to end is still an open question.
- **The know-how is a moat, not the code.** The moat is in stitching together the steps buyers hate: Form 145/146, the CA certificate, KYC on both sides, the FEMA limits and FINTRAC rules. A cross-border compliance setup is slow to copy, and competitors on the general remittance route have little reason to build it for a niche.
- **Timing.** The forms were just renamed from 15CA/15CB to 145/146, and India-side digital KYC rails (Setu, DigiLocker) exist. That gives a believable answer to "why now".
- **The secret is first-hand.** The founder has this problem himself. That is a real if thin edge in seeing what the bank desks get wrong.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No AD Category-I bank partner.** The transfer cannot legally run without one. The partner owns the rail, sets the fee (unknown) and could later offer the same service under its own name. Whoever controls the partner controls the monopoly, and today that is not REPAIHUB.
2. **No answer to the copycat question.** An Indian bank's NRI desk, a CA firm, or an app like Wise could add a digital NRO-outward flow. There is no network effect, no scale advantage yet and no brand. Is the product ten times better than a bank desk plus a CA, or just more convenient? Not proven.
3. **Distribution is a waitlist and one Instagram account.** The cost and channel for the first 100 customers are unknown. The budget is also unknown, and the founder works on this part-time with tight personal cash. On top of that, two different prices are in public (percentage pricing in the product, a flat $8–$15 on the landing page), which muddies the offer.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which AD bank are you talking to, what does it charge, and what stops it from cutting you out?
- Name every way a buyer does this today. Which one is closest, and why are you ten times better than it?
- What is the narrow beachhead: one city, one source of funds (property sales? inheritance?), one community channel?
- How do the first 100 customers arrive, and what does each one cost?
- How many waitlist sign-ups are there, and how many are ready to pay at the product's prices?
- What lasting asset do you build: data, a CA network, referral loops, a brand? What makes it hold for ten years?
- What is the startup budget, and how many months does it last?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A signed AD Category-I bank partnership with a known fee and terms that do not leave REPAIHUB easy to cut out.
2. FINTRAC MSB registration granted.
3. One price, the same in the product and on the landing page.
4. A named beachhead segment with evidence of demand at that price: paid pre-orders or committed sign-ups, plus a measured cost per customer.
5. A written plan for which durable advantage the business builds first.
