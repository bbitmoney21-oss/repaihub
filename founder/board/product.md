# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The one sentence already exists.** "Move your NRO savings to your Canadian bank, legally, paperwork done, no branch visit." It names a buyer, an outcome and the pain that is removed. That is rare at this stage.
- **It starts from a real moment.** The founder had this problem himself, and the brief describes the before clearly: the bank says "visit a branch in India", so you ask a friend. The product is shaped around getting rid of that moment.
- **The hard part is inside the product.** Forms 145/146 and the CA certificate are the part buyers dread, and the service does them. That is the right thing to own.
- **There is something to test.** A working web app with a demo mode means the experience can be put in front of customers now, not just described.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The core experience cannot be delivered yet.** No Authorised Dealer bank is signed, and without one the transfer cannot legally run. The product's promises (CAD in 24–48 h, Express in 8–12 h) depend on a partner whose speed, fee and process nobody knows. The experience was designed before the most important part of it exists.
2. **Seams everywhere, and nobody to catch what falls through them.** One transfer passes through Flinks/Fable, Setu, a PAN check, a partner CA, an AD bank and FINTRAC reporting. Each handoff is a place where a customer's money can sit stuck with no explanation. A solo founder with a full-time job, running it as "AI-operated", has not shown who handles a stuck ₹20L transfer at 2 a.m.
3. **The product contradicts itself before launch.** The landing page shows a flat $8/$12/$15, and the app charges 1–2% plus $24.99. Being honest about the price is part of the product, and a mismatch on money is how trust ends. The tiers are also hard to follow (5–10L costs more in percentage than under 5L), and the brief already adds inward transfers, Express and mobile. None of that is focus.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Walk me through the first five minutes, and then hour 30. What does the customer see while the money is in transit?
- Which AD bank, by when, and what does its process add to the timeline you promise?
- When the CA rejects a document or the bank holds a transfer, who tells the customer, how fast, and in what words?
- What would you cut? Why are inward transfers and Express in the first version?
- Can the pricing fit on one line a customer can check without a calculator?
- How many first transfers do you need to see go through end to end before you call the experience proven? (Open question: target not given.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A signed AD Category-I bank partner and FINTRAC registration, and the delivery-time promise rewritten to match the partner's real process.
2. One price structure, the same on every surface, explainable in one sentence.
3. Cut to outward NRO→Canada only. Inward transfers, Express and mobile come later.
4. A written exception path covering every handoff (rejected document, held transfer, KYC failure), with a named person who responds and a response time, tested on at least a handful of real transfers before opening the waitlist.
